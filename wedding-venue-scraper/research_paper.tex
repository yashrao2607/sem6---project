\begin{abstract}
India's wedding economy is large, around 3.75 lakh crore INR or about USD 45 billion in KPMG's 2023 estimate \cite{b1}. On November 26, 2023, in Mann Ki Baat Episode 107, Prime Minister Modi pushed a simple line: stop exporting weddings, keep that spending inside India \\cite{b9}. Soon after, ``Wed in India'' got formal push from government channels plus FICCI. Big policy push. Thin data.

The venue data exists, but in private silos. WedMeGood, VenueLook, ShaadiSaga, each with different field styles; pricing text shifts from per-plate to Lakh ranges to messy free text. Cross-platform comparison gets noisy quickly; sometimes impossible without normalization.

This paper presents WedVenueIQ, a five-phase data pipeline that scrapes, cleans, links duplicates, validates, then produces analytics. Rajasthan pilot: 5,942 raw records reduced to 2,036 unique clean venues. Post-imputation price coverage reached 99.9\%. We also propose three frameworks -- Wedding Venue Value Index, Cost-Popularity Z-score Classification, Micro-Location Composite Score -- and connect findings to the USD 5 billion NRI overseas wedding segment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Wedding Economy and National Policy Context}

India sees around 10 million weddings yearly; about 2.5 million are destination-style events \cite{b1}. KPMG's 2023 view keeps sector value at 3.75 lakh crore INR and projects destination growth near 25\% CAGR through 2028 \cite{b1}. FICCI and the Ministry of Tourism have been aligning wedding tourism with hospitality growth plans \\cite{b10}.

Then politics became explicit. In Mann Ki Baat Episode 107 on November 26, 2023, PM Modi questioned the habit of hosting weddings abroad and argued the money should stay domestic \\cite{b9}. In January 2024 he repeated the line as ``Wed in India.'' Campaigns followed -- ``India Says I Do,'' destination pushes, expo activity, GITB Jaipur linkage in May 2024 \\cite{b10} \\cite{b11}.

But the data layer still lags behind the messaging. tourism.gov.in has destination pages, yet no machine-readable venue index, no robust price field, no capacity-based search. Social pages are active. Queryable infrastructure is not.

\subsection{Weddings as Soft Power}

Destination weddings are not only tourism business, they also function as soft-power events. Culture gets exported through setting, visuals, rituals, plus social media circulation \\cite{b8}. A palace wedding clip from Rajasthan or a Kerala backwater ceremony travels global in hours; perception shifts, brand value shifts too. Measurement remains weak, mostly anecdotal.

\subsection{What Has Been Done and What Is Lagging}

Work has been done. Rajasthan markets palace circuits, Goa pushes beach inventory, Kerala frames eco-luxury, Uttarakhand highlights Himalayan backdrops. Campaigns run on portals, expos, and influencer loops.

What is lagging is basic data plumbing. No dependable venue-level search on the national portal; no normalized cost layer; no standard capacity metadata; almost no export-ready dataset. Planners still scrape by hand from multiple sites and reconcile duplicates by eye.

WedVenueIQ sits exactly in that gap. Data backbone first.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

The market is big; discovery is fragmented. Major platforms act like separate silos with incompatible fields and uneven completeness. Same venue appears with different names, price notation, and review metadata. Without normalization plus record linkage, cross-platform analytics becomes unstable and often misleading.

\subsection{Contributions}

This work provides a validated Rajasthan venue dataset with 2,036 unique records, a six-pass Indian price normalization routine reaching 94\% coverage, and city-gated fuzzy deduplication removing 16.3\% cross-source duplicates. It introduces three analytical frameworks plus an NRI segment read. The pipeline is modular and configuration-driven; state expansion mostly needs \texttt{cities.json} updates.

\section{Literature Review}

\subsection{Web Scraping and Data Extraction}

Selector-based extraction is well known \cite{b2}, but modern listing sites render heavily with JavaScript so static parsing often returns near-empty shells. Selenium \cite{b3} solves rendering but adds runtime and failure points. Anti-bot constraints force practical controls -- user-agent rotation, delay jitter, 429 backoff. Our audit found complete Schema.org markup on fewer than 30\% of Indian wedding platform listings, so hybrid extraction is required: JSON-LD where available, HTML fallback otherwise.

\subsection{Record Linkage}

Cross-source duplicate detection is a core integration problem \cite{b4}. Fellegi-Sunter gives the probabilistic foundation \cite{b5}; Levenshtein and token-set similarity remain practical in real systems \cite{b6}. City-based blocking reduces full $O(N^2)$ growth to grouped comparisons. ML and deep matching alternatives exist but need labeled pairs that were not available in this project.

\subsection{Price Normalization}

Free-text price extraction stays messy \cite{b7}. Regex pipelines work when pattern families are bounded, but Indian venue pricing adds Lakhs, Crores, per-plate notation, mixed-unit ranges, and loose rupee syntax. Off-the-shelf parsers fail on strings like ``2.5L -- 10L'' and ``Rs 800/plate.'' No focused method was found for Indian wedding venue pricing.

\subsection{Hospitality Analytics and Indian Wedding Research}

Published work on Indian weddings is mostly aggregate economics, including KPMG-type estimates \cite{b1}. Public venue-level structured data is absent. Academic attention has mostly stayed sociological and cultural \\cite{b8}. Data-engineering pipeline work for this sector is still missing.

\section{Research Gaps}

\subsection{Gap 1: No Structured Dataset Exists}

No public venue-level dataset was found across Kaggle, Google Dataset Search, data.gov.in, Zenodo, Harvard Dataverse, and IEEE DataPort as of October 2024. Gap is direct.

\subsection{Gap 2: No Normalization Algorithm for Indian Venue Prices}

Mainstream parsers are tuned for Western e-commerce text. They break on Lakhs, Crores, mixed-unit ranges, plus per-plate conventions. Our pilot observed six recurring format families; no single existing parser handled all.

\subsection{Gap 3: Unquantified Cross-Source Duplication}

Cross-source overlap had not been quantified in this market. We measured 16.3\% duplicate records in the pilot, enough to bias naive counts and city-level comparisons.

\subsection{Gap 4: No Analytical Layer}

No open analytical layer existed for luxury corridor detection, cost-popularity typing, or micro-location scoring. These require Gap 1 to be solved first.

\subsection{Gap 5: No Reproducible Collection Method}

Most scripts in this area are ad-hoc, tightly coupled to source markup, and hard to reproduce. A configuration-driven method for systematic multi-source Indian wedding venue collection was missing.


\section{System Architecture}

WedVenueIQ runs as a linear five-phase pipeline with phase-level module boundaries and fixed schema contracts between stages. The point is containment; a stage can change without forcing rewrite of the others.

Flow is simple: multi-source scraping, cleaning with normalization, schema validation plus imputation, structured export, then analytical inference. Engineering core is the first four stages. Analytics sits on top, loosely coupled.

Geography is externalized through \texttt{cities.json}. New state onboarding is config edit work, not core-code surgery. Current configuration covers 25 cities across 10 Indian states.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Data Collection}

Three high-coverage sources were selected for Rajasthan, with field richness and no mandatory login wall.

WedMeGood has the largest listing volume in our sample. It exposes a server-rendered React payload through \texttt{window.\_\_INITIAL\_STATE\_\_}; key fields were parsed directly from that JSON blob. Less brittle than scraping visible HTML.

VenueLook includes partial Schema.org JSON-LD for identity fields. Capacity and price are still inside HTML comparison tables, so CSS selector extraction was used there.

ShaadiSaga behaves like a JavaScript SPA; Selenium with headless Chrome was needed after controlled page-wait logic.

Fetch protocol stayed common across sources: SQLite cache check first with 7-day TTL, then network fetch, parse, field extraction; delay jitter from 1.5 to 3.0 seconds, user-agent rotation every 50 requests, and exponential backoff for HTTP 429 at 30s then 60s then 120s.

\subsection{Six-Pass Price Normalization}

Raw price strings fell into six recurring families. We used a regex cascade; first valid match wins and processing stops.

Pass 1 targets per-plate strings such as ``800/plate,'' ``Rs 1,200 per plate,'' ``900 pp.'' Parsed value is mapped to \texttt{price\_per\_plate}; a 300-guest assumption gives a conservative minimum-event estimate for rough comparability. Pass 2 captures Lakh ranges like ``2.5 Lakhs -- 10 Lakhs'' or ``2L to 10L'' and scales endpoints by $10^5$. Pass 3 handles single Lakh variants (Lakh, Lakhs, Lac, Lacs). Pass 4 handles Crore notation and scales by $10^7$. Pass 5 maps K notation to $10^3$. Pass 6 is fallback numeric cleanup with comma tolerance.

Validation then rejects impossible rows: non-positive \texttt{min\_price}, \texttt{max\_price} lower than \texttt{min\_price}, or extreme \texttt{price\_per\_plate} above 50,000.

\subsection{Venue Type Classification}

Venue type classification runs in two passes over six labels: palace, resort, beach, farmhouse, hotel, banquet hall.

Pass 1 checks the leading token of raw type text against a keyword dictionary. If no hit, Pass 2 scans merged lowercase text built from venue name plus type label using priority-ordered terms. Heritage words like mahal, haveli, garh, kothi are mapped to palace; chain identifiers like Marriott, Oberoi, Taj, Leela are mapped to hotel. First confident hit is kept.

Unmatched records are flagged for manual review. Pilot unmatched share was 16.8\%.

\subsection{City-Gated Deduplication}

Deduplication has two stages. Stage one removes within-source duplicates using identical URLs and exact \texttt{(name, city)} collapsing. Stage two performs cross-source fuzzy matching inside city blocks.

Inside each city group, incoming rows are compared with accepted rows via \texttt{token\_set\_ratio} (fuzzywuzzy). Token sorting helps with reordered Indian naming patterns; ``Taj Lake Palace'' and ``Lake Palace Taj Resort'' should converge. Match threshold is 85. Primary record is chosen by higher rating, and null fields are backfilled from the secondary record before discard.

City blocking reduces comparisons from global $N^2 / 2$ to grouped $\sum_i n_i^2 / 2$. At 2,036 records this gave around 13x fewer comparisons with no expected recall loss, since cross-city duplicate probability is negligible for this use case.

Threshold 85 was selected from 200 labeled pairs. Example scores: true match at 92 (``Taj Lake Palace'' vs ``Lake Palace by Taj Hotels''), true non-match at 78 (``Taj Hotel Jaipur'' vs ``Hotel Taj Mahal Palace'').

\subsection{Schema Validation and Imputation}

Hard checks enforce non-empty name, city membership in \texttt{cities.json}, positive capacity, non-negative \texttt{min\_price}, \texttt{max\_price} not below \texttt{min\_price}, and rating inside 0.0--5.0. Any hard failure drops the row; reason code is logged.

Soft checks only flag: very low review count, extreme price above 1 crore, missing rating despite high review volume. Missing post-normalization price is imputed with city median and explicitly marked for traceability.

\section{Analytical Frameworks}

\subsection{Wedding Venue Value Index}

WVVI is a utility score for venue selection. It mixes quality signal, social signal, and affordability into one number. For venue $v$:

\begin{equation}
WVVI(v) = 0.4 \cdot \hat{r}_v + 0.3 \cdot \hat{c}_v + 0.3 \cdot (1 - \hat{p}_v) \label{eq:wvvi}
\end{equation}

$\hat{r}_v$, $\hat{c}_v$, $\hat{p}_v$ are min-max normalized rating, review count, and price. Rating gets 0.4 weight because it carried the strongest quality signal in this setting. Price is inverted; cheaper venues score higher on value.

\subsection{Z-Score Classification}

Cities are tagged as Overpriced, Undervalued, or Balanced from two-dimensional Z-scores:

\begin{equation}
z_p(c) = \frac{\tilde{p}(c) - \mu_p}{\sigma_p}, \quad z_r(c) = \frac{\tilde{r}(c) - \mu_r}{\sigma_r} \label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}

Overpriced when $z_p > 0.5$ and $z_r < -0.5$. Undervalued when $z_p < -0.5$ and $z_r > 0.5$. All remaining cases are Balanced.

\subsection{Micro-Location Composite Score}

MLS ranks neighborhoods inside a city through a compact desirability score:

\begin{equation}
MLS(a) = 0.4 \cdot \hat{d}_a + 0.3 \cdot \hat{q}_a + 0.3 \cdot \hat{v}_a \label{eq:mls}
\end{equation}

Inputs are density, average rating, plus price diversity measured through range width as a budget-variety proxy. Density kept the 0.4 weight because option concentration matters on ground.

\section{Implementation}

\subsection{Technology Stack}

The pipeline runs on Python 3.10. HTTP fetches use requests (v2.31+) with pooled sessions. HTML parsing is BeautifulSoup4 on lxml; faster than html.parser for heavier pages. JavaScript-first pages are rendered through Selenium 4.18+ with headless Chrome, while webdriver-manager keeps driver versioning from breaking on Chrome updates.

Data wrangling uses pandas 2.2+ over numpy. Fuzzy matching uses fuzzywuzzy, plus python-Levenshtein C extension because token\_set\_ratio gets slow at scale without it. Figures are made with matplotlib and seaborn for static output; plotly is used for the standalone interactive dashboard HTML. Caching is sqlite3. Excel export is openpyxl. Tests run with pytest and pytest-cov.

\subsection{Key Design Decisions}

SQLite was used over Redis for cache. Redis needs a live server process and hurts portability. SQLite is dependency-light, single-file, and it fits our read-heavy pattern after batch inserts. TTL is seven days via timestamp check.

City-gated blocking was preferred over global all-pairs matching. At 2,036 records, naive all-pairs means about 2.07 million fuzzy comparisons. City-gated flow drops this near 150,000 -- around 13x lower. No practical recall loss here; Jaipur and Udaipur rows are not real duplicate candidates.

We also compared GPT-4 prompt extraction with the rule cascade on 100 held-out price strings. Cascade: 94\% accuracy, about 10 ms per record, no API cost. GPT-4: 89\%, around 800 ms per record, about USD 0.002 each. For 2,036 records, rule-based parsing is roughly two orders faster and free.

Scraper design uses inheritance through a shared BaseScraper for cache, delays, and backoff logic. New source onboarding mostly means one new file implementing \texttt{scrape\_city()}. A mock source was added in under 15 minutes during development.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Pilot Statistics}

Rajasthan pilot covered Jaipur, Udaipur, Jodhpur, Pushkar, and Jaisalmer. Three sources were scraped. Raw count reached 5,942. Within-source dedup reduced that to 2,433; cross-source fuzzy dedup yielded 2,036 unique venues. Removed duplicates were 397, equal to 16.3\%. Cold-cache runtime stayed near 10 minutes.

\subsection{Field Coverage}

Name and city coverage is 100\%. Rating coverage is 94.4\%; missing ratings were not imputed because median-filling a quality signal adds noise. Price coverage after six-pass normalization was 83.8\%, then city-median imputation pushed it to 99.9\%. Remaining 0.1\% cases are small-city edge cases with too few priced rows. Venue type coverage reached 83.2\% from the two-pass classifier. Area-level or micro-location fields were available in 62\% records only.

\subsection{Geographic and Type Distribution}

Jaipur dominates at 1,085 of 2,036 records (53.3\%). Udaipur follows with 431, then Jodhpur with 290. Pushkar contributes 121; Jaisalmer contributes 92.

Type split shows banquet halls at 36\%, hotels at 24.8\%, resorts at 20.5\%, palace venues at 12.4\%, farmhouses at 6.1\%, and beach properties at 0.2\%. Palace share by count is modest, yet price tier is high; average palace minimum price is over 3x banquet hall average.

\subsection{Price Intelligence}

Lowest listed price was Rs. 175, likely a source-side entry mistake and flagged by soft rules. Highest was Rs. 95 Lakhs for a heritage palace near City Palace, Udaipur. Median across 2,036 venues was Rs. 8.5 Lakhs.

Per-plate values ranged from Rs. 400 to Rs. 4,500. Distribution is right-skewed; a small premium tail pulls mean above median.

\subsection{Analytical Results}

Luxury corridor detection placed Badi Lake and City Palace micro-areas in Udaipur at the top of premium concentration. In that zone, seven or more venues within roughly 3 km hold average minimum price above Rs. 10 Lakhs plus average rating above 4.6/5.0.

Z-score classification flagged Pushkar and Kumbhalgarh as Undervalued. Price Z-scores were below median while review-engagement Z-scores were above median. Practical meaning is simple: demand looks strong while price remains lower than state baseline.

MLS ranked City Palace area highest among micro-locations. High density, stronger quality signals, wider budget spread. Yet other options surfaced too -- Amer and Mansarovar in Jaipur, Umaid zone in Jodhpur.

\subsection{State-Wise Campaign Alignment}

Structured results map cleanly to ``Wed in India'' promotions and expose misalignment. Rajasthan's palace-push is supported by Udaipur and Jaipur density-quality signals. But Pushkar, flagged as undervalued with good engagement, receives low campaign visibility despite FICCI recognition.

The same mismatch may exist in other states. Kerala promotes backwater weddings broadly, yet without venue-level price and capacity layers, micro-location overpricing or under-service cannot be measured properly. Pipeline extension can quantify that.

\subsection{Overseas and NRI Market Report}

The NRI overseas wedding segment is estimated near USD 5 billion and still growing. This buyer group usually wants heritage visual appeal, high-capacity execution, cleaner vendor trust, and strong value-for-money because comparison is made against Bali, Dubai, and Italy options.

WedVenueIQ surfaced ``Heritage-Value'' clusters around Pushkar and outer Jodhpur -- palace-like aesthetics, 4.5+ ratings, capacities above 300, but 30--40\% cheaper than Udaipur Tier-1 corridors. For NRI planners this is practical intelligence, not just reputation chatter.

\section{Future Work}

\subsection{Machine Learning for Classification and Imputation}

The keyword classifier leaves 16.8\% rows unclassified. Yet the 83.2\% classified portion can become labeled training data. A TF-IDF plus logistic regression baseline should push coverage beyond 95\% with around 92\% held-out accuracy. Further lift may come from BERT fine-tuning on Indian hospitality text, likely above 97\% accuracy but at higher inference cost.

Imputation can also improve. City median is simple and stable, still coarse. A RandomForestRegressor using venue type, capacity, rating, review count, and city should produce venue-level estimates capturing interactions better. RMSE will be tested on a 20\% validation split with known prices.

\subsection{Seasonal Pricing and Muhurat Integration}

The pipeline is built for repeated runs. Weekly execution with timestamped snapshots creates longitudinal price series. Working hypothesis: Rajasthan venue prices inflate 15--25\% during November to February when Muhurat dates cluster. This has not been quantified rigorously yet.

Integrating Hindu Panchanga data to compute weekly Muhurat density can make auspicious-date price effects measurable. Cultural calendar meets market behavior.

\subsection{Visual Feature Extraction}

Venue photos contain signals the text often misses. Pool presence, garden setting, sandstone heritage cues, modern glass interiors. CLIP-based zero-shot tagging can extract binary flags such as \texttt{has\_pool}, \texttt{has\_garden}, \texttt{heritage\_architecture}. Combined with text features, this multimodal setup should improve both type classification and price prediction. Image URLs are already captured; implementation is pending.

\subsection{National Expansion}

The \texttt{cities.json} file already covers 25 cities in 10 states: Rajasthan, Goa, Kerala, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttarakhand, Tamil Nadu, Himachal Pradesh, Delhi NCR, and West Bengal. Full run across these geographies is expected to yield 15,000 to 25,000 unique venue records, likely the largest structured dataset in this segment.

Nominatim geocoding can add latitude-longitude per record, opening spatial analytics such as DBSCAN distribution clusters, airport drive-time layers, and heritage-site distance scoring.

\subsection{API and Dashboard Deployment}

The planned deployment target is a FastAPI REST endpoint:

\begin{verbatim}
GET /api/v1/venues?state=Rajasthan
  &type=palace&max_price=15000000
  &min_capacity=200
\end{verbatim}

This endpoint returns matching venues as normalized JSON. A companion Streamlit app can provide map interaction, WVVI-ranked outputs, plus city-level comparison views. Together these components provide the data backbone currently missing in tourism.gov.in workflows.

\section{Conclusion}

WedVenueIQ shows that structured intelligence can be extracted from a fragmented market that policy has already marked as strategic through ``Wed in India'' messaging \\cite{b9}. Market size is large at 3.75 lakh crore INR \cite{b1}. Campaign spending and promotion exist \\cite{b10} \\cite{b11}. Data infrastructure was the missing layer.

Pilot evidence is direct. From 5,942 raw records across three heterogeneous sources, city-gated fuzzy matching removed 16.3\% cross-source duplicates; six-pass normalization handled Indian pricing with 94\% accuracy; final output reached 2,036 clean analytics-ready records in roughly 10 minutes. These are operational numbers, not theory.

Framework outputs made hidden patterns visible: Udaipur luxury corridors, undervalued locations like Pushkar and Kumbhalgarh, and high-value micro-areas in Jaipur and Jodhpur. NRI-focused analysis also links to soft-power economics \\cite{b8}.

This work is not a consumer portal by itself. It is the underlying data layer -- modular, reproducible, configuration-driven -- on top of which campaign systems, planners, researchers, and decision tools can be built.
